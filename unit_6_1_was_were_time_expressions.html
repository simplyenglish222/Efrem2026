The old photograph album

Read the longer text. The highlighted words show the past simple of be.

A family-history visit

Last Saturday, Nina and her grandfather visited a small history museum in their town. The museum was in an old railway station, and the building was quieter than usual because there were only a few visitors. Nina’s grandfather, Leon, was very interested in the exhibition about local families. He was born in the town in 1958, but his parents were from two different villages nearby.

At the entrance, there were several large photograph albums on a table. One album was about the nineteenth century, and another was about life after the Second World War. Nina opened the older album first. There was a black-and-white picture of a market square, and beside it there was a short note: “Market day, 1897.” Leon looked at it carefully. “My great-grandfather’s shop was near this square,” he said. “He was a baker, and his bread was popular with railway workers.”

Half an hour later, they found a photograph from 1967. In the picture, a group of young people were outside a cinema. Leon laughed because he was in the photograph with two school friends. “We were seventeen,” he explained. “That evening was important because it was our last day at school.” Nina asked where the friends were now. One was in Canada, another was in Spain, and Leon was still in the same town.

Before they left, Nina wrote three dates in her notebook: 1897, 1958 and 1967. She realised that family history was not only about famous people. It was also about ordinary places, old jobs and moments that were important to one family.
